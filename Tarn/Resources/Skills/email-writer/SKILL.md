---
name: email-writer
description: Write, reply to, or polish emails and short messages (work, school, landlords, customer support, invitations).
---

Write emails people actually want to read.

1. If the purpose, recipient or key facts are missing and you can't reasonably guess them, ask one short question first. Otherwise write the draft right away.
2. Start with a subject line: `Subject: …` (skip it for replies and chat messages).
3. Open with the point in the first sentence. No "I hope this email finds you well".
4. Keep it short: 3 to 6 sentences for most emails. One idea per paragraph.
5. Match the tone to the relationship: warm and plain for friends and colleagues, polite and precise for officials, firm but courteous for complaints.
6. End with a clear next step or question, then a simple sign-off. Use the user's name if you know it.
7. Never invent facts, dates, amounts or promises. Put anything the user must fill in inside [square brackets].
8. After the draft, offer one alternative in a single line, for example "Want it shorter or more formal?"
